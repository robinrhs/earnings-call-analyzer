Lena Brandt -- Head of Investor Relations

Thank you, operator, and good morning, everyone. With me today are Dana Whitfield, our Chief Executive Officer, and Martin Okafor, our Chief Financial Officer. Before we begin, please note that today's discussion contains forward-looking statements that are subject to risks and uncertainties, and actual results may differ materially. We will also refer to non-GAAP measures such as adjusted operating margin and free cash flow; reconciliations are in the earnings release. With that, I will hand it to Dana.

Dana Whitfield -- Chief Executive Officer

Thank you, Lena, and good morning. The third quarter was our strongest quarter to date. Revenue grew 14% year over year to $412.3 million, ahead of the top end of the range we gave you in July. Demand for our warehouse automation systems remained healthy in North America, and our recurring software business continued to compound.

Annual recurring revenue from our Fernhill Orchestrate software platform reached $588 million at quarter end, up 22% from a year ago. Software now represents a little over a third of total revenue, and every system we install creates a long tail of subscription and service revenue. That mix shift is the single most important driver of our margin story.

Orders were also strong. We booked $445 million of new orders in the quarter, which translates into a book-to-bill ratio of 1.08, and our backlog closed the quarter at a record $1.21 billion. Roughly 60% of that backlog is scheduled to convert to revenue over the next twelve months, which gives us good visibility into the fourth quarter and into fiscal 2027.

Among the new orders, we signed two multi-site agreements with North American third-party logistics providers, each covering more than ten distribution centers, and our first project with a regional pharmaceutical distributor. Those wins matter because they are repeatable: once a customer has standardized on Fernhill at a few sites, the next sites are faster to sell and cheaper to install.

I do want to be clear about two areas where conditions are tougher. First, Europe. Order intake from European grocery and apparel customers slowed in the quarter, and several customers pushed final investment decisions into next year. European orders were down 9% year over year. We are not seeing cancellations, but decision cycles are clearly longer, and we are planning conservatively for the region.

Second, component costs. New tariffs on imported motion-control components took effect in August. We have secured supply for the next two quarters, but we pre-bought inventory to do so, and that ties up cash. We expect to offset most of the cost impact through pricing and sourcing changes by the middle of next year, but there will be a timing gap.

Strategically, we launched Orchestrate 5 in September, which adds predictive maintenance and labor planning modules. Early adoption has been encouraging, with 41 existing customers signing upgrades in the first four weeks. We also opened our new integration center in Monterrey, Mexico, which will shorten installation times for customers in North America.

On capital allocation, our priorities have not changed. We invest first in research and development and in our installation capacity, then in small, software-focused acquisitions, and we return excess cash to shareholders. During the quarter we repurchased $12 million of shares under the $50 million program the board approved in March.

With that, let me hand over to Martin for the financial details.

Martin Okafor -- Chief Financial Officer

Thanks, Dana, and good morning, everyone. As Dana said, third quarter revenue was $412.3 million, up 14% from $361.7 million in the prior-year quarter. Systems revenue was $268.1 million, up 10%, and software and services revenue was $144.2 million, up 23%.

By region, North America grew 21% and represented 68% of revenue. Europe declined 4% and represented 24% of revenue, and the rest of the world made up the remaining 8%. Because European projects are recognized over the installation period, the slowdown in European orders shows up in revenue with a lag of two to three quarters.

Gross margin expanded to 47.8%, compared with 46.1% a year ago. The improvement came from the richer software mix and better project execution, partly offset by about 40 basis points of tariff-related component costs. Adjusted operating margin was 15.2%, up from 13.9% in the third quarter of last year. Adjusted earnings per share were $0.74, compared with $0.61 a year ago.

Operating expenses were $134.4 million, or 32.6% of revenue, compared with 32.2% a year ago. The increase reflects higher research and development spending ahead of the Orchestrate 5 launch and the start-up costs of the Monterrey integration center. We expect operating expenses as a percentage of revenue to come down in the fourth quarter as those one-off costs roll off.

Free cash flow was $38.5 million in the quarter. That is lower than we would normally expect at this revenue level, and the reason is the inventory build Dana described. We added roughly $31 million of component inventory ahead of the tariff changes. We ended the quarter with $214 million of cash and a net leverage ratio of 0.9 times adjusted EBITDA.

Now to our outlook. Based on the strength of our backlog, we are raising our fiscal 2026 revenue guidance to a range of $1.62 billion to $1.66 billion, up from our prior range of $1.58 billion to $1.63 billion. We continue to expect an adjusted operating margin of approximately 15% for the full year.

For the fourth quarter, we expect revenue in the range of $435 million to $450 million.

We are lowering our fiscal 2026 free cash flow guidance to $120 million to $135 million, compared with our previous range of $140 million to $155 million. The reduction reflects the inventory investment and the timing of tariff costs, not a change in the underlying cash generation of the business. We expect working capital to normalize during the first half of fiscal 2027.

With that, operator, please open the line for questions.
